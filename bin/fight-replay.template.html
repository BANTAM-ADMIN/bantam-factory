    what BANTAM is

    A harness for small models running on your own machine. The model here is a 27B open-weights model on one GPU — the same weights the HERMES and OPENCODE corners drive.

    BANTAM gives it a constrained action protocol, a workspace it cannot wander out of, and a line of stations — small jigs that catch a known failure the moment it starts, the way a factory catches a defect at the station that makes it rather than at final inspection.

    why it is faster on the same weights

    • on this board the prompt was built so the model's cache survives the turn rather than being rewritten (--context-mode extension) — note this is not the shipped default, which rebuilds the prompt each turn and scores higher per run
    • actions are schema-constrained, so a malformed step is impossible rather than retried
    • stations stop the loops that eat the clock: re-reading, re-patching, oscillating edits
    • the run records itself, so every claim on this page can be re-derived from bytes

    Open source, Apache-2.0. A card is one command: bantam fight "<task>".

    Honest limits, stated on purpose: these are curated tasks, not a public leaderboard, and the field is what one workstation can run — a 27B local model, two rival local harnesses on the identical weights, and frontier agents through their own CLIs. Frontier corners answer over a network from far larger models; they are here for calibration, not as a like-for-like weight class. Every card on this board carries the full roster, and got there by being run: a corner is never fabricated. Three early bouts whose workspaces were generated at fight time and not kept could not be completed honestly, so their briefs were rebuilt as reproducible kits and refought in full (cards 32 and 33); the originals keep their records but leave the board. Where a corner hit the wall or built nothing, the card says so instead of dropping the row. What was fought: the BANTAM corners ran a Qwen 3.8 27B fine-tune (Apache-2.0, Q4_K_M) rather than the stock conversion the installer fetches, and in --context-mode extension, which is not the shipped default. The harness is model-agnostic — any Qwen 3.x GGUF, or any OpenAI-compatible endpoint, drives the same loop — but a different model or mode will not reproduce these exact numbers. Extension buys prefix-cache reuse at a measured cost in per-run reliability (22/30 vs rebuild’s 30/30 on the preregistered compact-strictness family, replicated three times). Both change the numbers on this page; a stock-weights, default-mode re-fight is an open item.
