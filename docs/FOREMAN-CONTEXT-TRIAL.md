# Foreman context trial: accepted completion, not a speed claim

The fourth private context-packet treatment used harness commit `61a0e94`,
Astra supervision, one serial local 27B BANTAM lane and one shared Terra lane.
It finished with an accepted supervisor completion, 18/18 integrated tests,
5/5 independent grading groups, unchanged protected starter materials and
confirmed owned-container cleanup. End-to-end time was **334.022 seconds**.
Raw prompts, candidate snapshots and account receipts remain private.

## What changed and what was observed

The preceding treatment reached supervisor finish with 15 passing tests, but
teardown threw before saving the consolidated result. A separate recovery
grade of its retained candidate subsequently passed 5/5. That does not rewrite
the original infrastructure failure into a successful run.

Its local repair context also exposed specific factory defects:

- A diagnostic reproduction filename became a false required deliverable.
- A background mention of optimal context, combined with array slots elsewhere
  in the task, triggered an unrelated search obligation.
- Node module-mode computation was not recognized by the computation detector.
- Additional regression testing found multi-dot deliverable filenames could
  be truncated by the path extractor.

The new handoff preserves diagnostic evidence in model context separately from
the binding task consumed by requirement classifiers. The gate fixes retain
genuine output and computation checks. Completion is checkpointed before
teardown; exact-ID cleanup records include removal attempts and independent
absence inspection. Unconfirmed cleanup still prevents an overall pass.

The fourth treatment's two local jobs used 14 and 17 turns respectively. Each
completed on its first `done` attempt, without the prior false search or
missing-output bounces. This is an observed removal of those symptoms, not a
controlled estimate of their isolated latency effect.

## Quality and coordination

Astra detected missing-own-array-slot validation, unsafe CLI entry-path
comparison and potential large-output truncation, then commissioned local
regressions and repairs. It also diagnosed a Terra-authored test whose
`structuredClone` baseline lost a null prototype and falsely reported mutation.
The two repairs ran in parallel with separate edit ownership and focused
verification, avoiding dependence on another worker's known-broken test.

All four jobs passed. Local job occupation was 114.205s and 113.152s; Terra job
occupation was 112.777s and 42.846s. Occupation includes worker process and
verification overhead, not just model generation. Those intervals overlap.

## Interpretation and next experiments

This establishes an end-to-end supervised completion with useful independent
review. It does **not** establish a speed or token-efficiency advantage. The
ordinary BANTAM context-packet baseline was 84.325s and native Astra 112.567s;
both passed the original five grading groups. Different trajectories and
additional review coverage limit what a single comparison can establish.

The next efficiency targets are evidence-bearing wakeups instead of repeated
paid wait decisions, smaller verifiable worker checks instead of broad scratch
scripts, and explicit repair ownership/snapshot freshness. Keep audit and
verification requirements: reduce irrelevant work, not the quality threshold.

A local 27B supervisor with a distinct worker context is a future treatment,
not an implemented mode or measured claim. It can reuse the same queue and
verification architecture, serializing local supervisor/worker inference and
optionally escalating to authorized cloud workers. Factory self-repair should
produce tested versioned patches, followed by fresh validation; never silently
modify the harness or grader of an in-progress measurement.
